Benthamite reasoning shows up in policy areas where the goal is to raise the overall average, even if gains aren't evenly spread. Common examples include:

- **Broad tax cuts** that stimulate general economic activity and consumer spending
- **Universal infrastructure investment** (roads, broadband) that raises productivity nationwide
- **Public health campaigns** targeting the whole population rather than high-risk groups specifically

### The Common Thread
Each of these policies is judged a success if the *national average* Life Satisfaction rises — regardless of whether the gains are concentrated among people who were already comfortable.

> **Side Note: When Benthamite Logic Backfires**
> A stimulus policy that overwhelmingly benefits high earners can raise the average while leaving the bottom of the distribution untouched — or even worsening relative deprivation for those left behind.
