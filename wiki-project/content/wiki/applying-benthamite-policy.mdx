Benthamite thinking shows up whenever a policy is judged by whether the overall average goes up. The gains do not have to be shared evenly. Common examples include:

- **Broad tax cuts**, which boost spending and economic activity across the income scale
- **Corporate deregulation**, which gives a big boost to top earners but can weaken protections for the lowest earners
- **Universal infrastructure investment** (roads, broadband, transport links), which helps productivity and daily life across the country
- **Public health campaigns aimed at everyone**, like general anti-smoking adverts, rather than at high-risk groups

### The Common Thread
Each of these policies passes one test: **does the national average Life Satisfaction go up?** It does not matter whether the gains go to people who were already comfortable or are spread out evenly.
