Rawlsian reasoning shows up in policy areas explicitly targeted at raising the minimum, even at some cost to those already doing well. Common examples include:

- **Means-tested welfare payments** directed only at those below a poverty threshold
- **Minimum wage legislation** that raises the wage floor without directly affecting high earners
- **Targeted social housing** for the most vulnerable, funded through progressive taxation

### The Common Thread
Each of these policies is judged a success if the **floor** rises — the position of the average, or of already-comfortable citizens, is secondary.

> **Side Note: The Trade-off**
> Rawlsian policies can be expensive relative to their effect on the national average, since resources are deliberately concentrated on a smaller, worse-off group rather than spread broadly.
