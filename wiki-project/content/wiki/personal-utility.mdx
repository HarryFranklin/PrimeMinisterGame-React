**Personal Utility** measures how a person values changes in their *own* Life Satisfaction. It comes from the same kind of gamble as Societal Utility, but this time the choice is about your own life: a sure outcome, or a treatment that might make things better or worse.

### People Take More Risks for Themselves
Most people **choose more riskily for themselves than they would for others**. Faced with the same odds, they are more willing to gamble with their own life than with the lives of a group of citizens. Put the other way round, people are more cautious with other people's lives than with their own.

Because people are less cautious about their own lives, the Personal Utility curve is less steep at the bottom. Lifting the worst-off still counts for more than adding to people who are already doing well, but by less than under Societal Utility.

A government that follows this idea judges each policy by how each citizen values the change in their own life. The measure it aims to raise is the **National Personal Satisfaction (NPS)**.

### Comparing the Two Curves
The chart below puts both curves on the same axes. They mostly agree about gains near the top. They split apart at the bottom, where Societal Utility gives the worst-off much more weight.

One way to see the gap is to score each curve out of 10 points and ask how many points lifting one person from 2 to 4 is worth. Under Societal Utility it is worth about **7.2** points. Under Personal Utility it is worth about **5.5**. If every point counted the same, as in the Benthamite approach, it would be worth **2.5** (2 of the 8 steps from 2 to 10).

> **Side Note: What Real People Think**  
> A UK study asked 300 adults to make choices about risks to their own lives and about outcomes for society. About 4 in 5 (79%) were cautious in choices about their own lives. Nearly 9 in 10 (87%) were cautious in choices about society. Almost three quarters (73%) were more cautious when choosing for society than when choosing for themselves. These views were not linked to how people voted. (These figures leave out choices involving death.)

### The Case for Personal Utility
Personal Utility **respects the choices people make for their own lives**. Nobody knows your life better than you do. If you would accept a risk for yourself, why should a government be more cautious on your behalf than you would be?

### The Dilemma
So a government that wants to use utility has to choose. Societal Utility gives the worst-off more weight than Personal Utility does, so the two can recommend different policies. The next page shows this happening.
