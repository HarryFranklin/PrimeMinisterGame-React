In response to the failures of simple averages, the Rawlsian approach argues that a society should be judged by how it treats its most vulnerable citizens. 

Rather than aiming to raise the average, this framework directs all political capital toward raising the **Minimum Wellbeing Baseline**. Policies are designed to protect the worst-off, even if doing so limits gains at the top of the distribution.

> **Side Note: The Veil of Ignorance**  
> John Rawls proposed the "veil of ignorance" thought experiment: if you did not know where in society you would be placed, you would rationally choose to design a system that prioritises the worst-off.

### The Issue with Linearity
The Rawlsian framework successfully addresses absolute poverty, but it relies on a flawed mathematical assumption: that the life satisfaction scale is perfectly linear. 

It treats a jump from 2 to 3 out of 10 exactly the same as a jump from 8 to 9. However, human experience dictates that escaping misery is vastly more valuable than gaining extra luxury. To account for this, we must introduce the concept of Utility.
