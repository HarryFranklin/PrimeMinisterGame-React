1. MAO Engine and Optimal Path Calculation

The MAO (maximum achievable outcome) is the absolute highest score possible for a given cycle given the policies in place - acting as the mathematical ceiling.
- The Problem:
    - Every turn, the player is presented with eight hidden policy options in the "in-tray" area, and there are five per cycle, so there are 8^5 pathways a player can take.
- Beam Search Optimisation:
    - To calculate the absolute best path without freezing the browser, the MAO engine uses a beam search. 
    - Instead of exploring all paths to the end, it evaluates the immmedate impact of the 8 policies on turn one, sorts them by score, and only explores the top 3 policies.
- The Result:
    - By repeating this process, the engine navigates down to the optimal combination of five policies.
    - The final score of the perfect path becomes the cycleMAO, and the sequence of policies becomes the optimalPath (seen in Dev Panel).

2. The Difficulty Calculation (Target Score)

If the game demanded perfection, players would have to match the MAO exactly to win, which would be incredibly frustrating. 
The difficulty calculation gives them a margin for error.
- The Scalar:
    - In utils/frameworkRules.ts, each framework has a winThresholdScalar (e.g., 0.95 for Benthamite, 0.65 for Rawlsian).
- Target Threshold:
    - The engine multiplies the cycleMAO by this scalar to determine the actual passing grade. 
    - If the Benthamite MAO is 9.444 and the scalar is 0.95, the target score becomes roughly 8.97.
- Approval Rating Translation:
    - The WelfareMetrics.calculateApprovalRating function uses this target. If the player's final score meets or exceeds this target-
    - The game translates their score into an approval rating of 51.0% or higher, securing a win.

3. The Dev Panel Simulator

The "Run Simulator" button tests how easily a player could accidentally stumble into a win without engaging with the learning objectives.
- Deterministic Nature:
    - When clicked, it generates the exact sequence of policies that the player will get, using the same deterministic seed logic.
- Random Walk:
    - It plays 10,000 dummy games for each cycle and blindly picks 1 of the available policies randomly per turn.
- Calculating the Probability:
    - It compares the final score of each random walk against the target score. 
    - If 1,800 out of 10,000 random walks beat the target, the simulator outputs an 18% random win chance.
